"§4 · LAYER 1\nCurrency Constituency Engine\nadaptive membership · hysteresis-gated\n\nThe Constituency Engine is Layer 1 of the three-layer architecture (§2.2). MTQΣ maintains an eligible universe and independently decides which currencies qualify for the active basket. Membership is an algorithmic property with hysteresis, so the system can admit a rising currency or retire a deteriorating one without discretionary intervention and without thrashing at the boundary. The engine is evaluated on the strategic review cadence (quarterly by default, §10.10) and on structural-change events. On this pilot, admission remains governed — the universe below is validation-stage; no membership change has ever been executed.\n\n§4.2 · ELIGIBILITY SCORING\nThe Eligibility Score Q_i\n8 components · 6 positive · 2 negative\n\nFor every candidate currency i, the engine computes a composite eligibility score from six positive and two negative components. A currency becomes eligible for the active basket only if Q_i ≥ Q_min.\n\nQ_i = a_R·R_i + a_T·T_i + a_L·L_i + a_S·S_i + a_D·D_i + a_C·C_i − a_G·G_i − a_X·X_i\nComponent\tSymbol\tSign\tMeasured property\nReserve relevance\tR_i\t+ positive\tShare and persistence of currency i in global official reserves (IMF COFER).\nTrade / payment relevance\tT_i\t+ positive\tInternational trade invoicing, payment usage and cross-border settlement volume.\nMarket liquidity\tL_i\t+ positive\tFX turnover, market depth, bid-ask spread quality, settlement availability.\nStability characteristics\tS_i\t+ positive\tMulti-horizon volatility and purchasing-power stability (Section 5.1).\nDiversification contribution\tD_i\t+ positive\tMarginal risk reduction contributed to the basket portfolio.\nConvertibility / depth\tC_i\t+ positive\tConvertibility, market depth and transferability of the currency.\nGeopolitical concentration risk\tG_i\t− negative\tConcentration of issuing jurisdiction in sanctions, freeze or conflict exposure.\nCapital-control / transferability risk\tX_i\t− negative\tRisk of capital controls or transferability restrictions.\nScore normalization — §4.2.1\n\nRaw components are measured in different units (reserve shares in percent, turnover in trillions, volatility in annualized decimal), so Q_i is normalized before threshold comparison — otherwise datasets with different units silently distort the score [COO-1]. Default: min–max across the candidate universe; a percentile or rank transformation is an approved bounded alternative for heavy-tailed distributions or small universes. Whichever transform is selected becomes part of the published methodology version (§24.1).\n\nQ̃_i = (Q_i − Q_min) / (Q_max − Q_min)\n\nThe coefficients a_k and the threshold Q_min are methodology parameters — fixed per published methodology version; any change requires Risk Council approval through the parameter-registry process (§22.3). The coefficients are themselves subject to methodology validation (§23.6), and the entire scoring table is published with each weight publication (§24.3) so eligibility decisions remain reproducible.\n\nStructural score construction — §4.5\n\nEvery input is normalized before aggregation [MS §8]. The default transform is the z-score, where μ_k and σ_k are the cross-sectional mean and standard deviation of input k across the candidate universe:\n\nz_i,k = (x_i,k − μ_k) / σ_k\n\nA bounded percentile or rank transform is substituted when distributional stability is preferred — when an input is heavy-tailed or the universe is small enough that a single outlier would dominate the z-score. The composite structural score is then:\n\nS_i^Structural = ∑_k a_k · z_i,k\n\nThe aggregation weights a_k are themselves subject to methodology validation: the sensitivity of the final basket to each a_k is tested by parameter perturbation (§23.6), and unstable coefficients are re-derived or the input is re-transformed. This closes the loop — the constituency engine is as auditable as the weighting engine, because every intermediate score is published with the weights (§24.3). Pilot honesty: the Q_i model is validation-stage (spec-plus-live-COFER inputs); membership changes have never been executed.\n\n§4.3 · INSTITUTIONAL DATA\nInstitutional Data Sources\ninputs — never weights\nIMF COFER — reserve relevance (R_i)\n\nThe Currency Composition of Official Foreign Exchange Reserves (COFER) is the primary reserve-relevance input. It currently identifies USD, EUR, CNY, JPY, GBP, AUD, CAD, CHF as reported reserve currencies, while explicitly excluding monetary gold from the foreign-exchange-reserve classification. Two implications follow: gold's reserve role is measured by supplementary sources (central-bank gold statistics) rather than COFER, and any currency absent from COFER carries a structurally reduced R_i until reporting begins. COFER is updated quarterly, which sets the natural cadence for reserve-relevance refreshes.\n\nBIS Triennial Survey 2025 — liquidity & trade (L_i, T_i)\nUSD\n89.2%\nEUR\n28.9%\nJPY\n16.8%\nGBP\n10.2%\nCNY\n8.5%\nCHF\n6.4%\n\nShare of one side of FX turnovers. COFER and BIS turnover shares feed R_i, L_i and T_i through the scoring model of §4.2 — the published weight of any currency is set by the MASE pipeline (§24.5), never copied from a survey share. For an entering currency j, the weight ramps over a transition window:\n\nW_j,t = r_t · W_j,target\nr_t = t/T — linear ramp is the default.\nWindow length T is adjusted for the entering component's liquidity and prevailing market conditions — replacing the old fixed 30-day rule — and may not be shortened below a constitutional minimum.\nThe ramp feeds through the same MARP execution gates as any other weight change: a transition is a sequence of justified partial corrections, not a block trade.\nTwo-threshold dead band — §4.7\nentry: Q_i > Q_entry\nexit: Q_i < Q_exit\nwith Q_entry > Q_exit (dead band)\n\nA currency that has just entered must see its score fall below the lower exit threshold before retirement can begin; a currency that has exited must exceed the higher entry threshold before re-admission. The dead-band width is a Risk-Council parameter bounded by a constitutional minimum, and both thresholds are published. Hysteresis composes with the rebalancing direction lock (§11.5) and the smoothing rule (§8.4) to give the basket three independent anti-oscillation defenses, each operating at a different timescale.\n\n§4.4 · CONSTITUTIONAL NEUTRALITY\nNon-Decisions Are Constitutional\ncases A–D · no pre-bias\n\nThe initial serious universe is USD, EUR, JPY, CNY, GBP, CHF, with CAD, AUD, SGD and other liquid currencies as monitored candidates [MS §7]. Two explicit non-decisions are constitutional: GBP is not automatically removed, and CHF is not automatically guaranteed admission. The mathematical process must be able to produce any of the following outcomes, and the architecture must not pre-bias any of them:\n\nCase\tOutcome\tMeaning\nA\tQ_GBP > Q_CHF\tSterling outranks the franc on the composite score.\nB\tQ_CHF > Q_GBP\tThe franc outranks sterling on the composite score.\nC\tBoth GBP and CHF exceed Q_min\tBoth currencies earn inclusion.\nD\tA monitored candidate (CAD/AUD/SGD/…) exceeds Q_min\tA new currency qualifies for admission via the transition rules of Section 4.6.\n\nThe same neutrality applies to the incumbent six: eligibility is re-tested on every review, and a currency whose Q_i falls below the exit threshold enters the retirement path (§4.6) with the same gradual transitions and the same anti-oscillation hysteresis as any other component.\n\nSGTX-G is not inherited automatically — §4.8\n\nBecause this project is MTQΣ, any physical-gold token must be designed under MTQΣ's own legal and monetary architecture [MS §57]. The earlier SGTX-G concept (a 1-gram physical-gold representation) is historical design input only. Concretely: gold exposure in the reference basket is to the gold price reference unit (§17.4), not to any specific token — the reserve implementation of gold chooses among eligible instruments (PAXG, XAUT, physical bullion, or a future MTQ-native instrument) under the Chapter 15 eligibility framework.
