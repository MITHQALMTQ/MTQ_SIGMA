Monte Carlo testing generates N simulated paths under multiple distributions and regimes, and tests the stability of the live weight trajectory W_t under path variations [MS §83]. Each family is constructed so that it attacks a different assumption of the engine, and each generator is versioned with the methodology so results are reproducible (§24.1):\n\nFamily\tGenerator\tFailure assumption attacked\nHistorical block bootstrap\tResampled blocks of joint currency/gold returns, preserving short-horizon dependence.\tThat the future resembles reordered history.\nParametric Gaussian\tMultivariate normal with the shrunk covariance of §6.2.\tBehavior under idealized, thin-tailed dynamics.\nFat-tailed\tMultivariate Student-t with heavy degrees of freedom.\tThat tails are Gaussian-sized.\nRegime-switching\tMarkov switching among the six regimes of §5.4 with stress transitions.\tThat regimes arrive gently and rarely.\nJump / crisis\tJump-diffusion paths with synchronized FX and gold jumps.\tThat moves are continuous.\nCorrelation collapse\tPaths with ρ_ij → 1.\tThat diversification persists when needed (§23.9).\nPurchasing-power drift\tPaths with persistent divergence between currency returns and the GPP index.\tThat purchasing-power tracking is easy.\n\nFor each family the program reports the dispersion of the weight trajectories, the distribution of turnover and execution cost, the drawdown distribution against the constitutional MDD envelope, and the reserve coverage distribution (§23.11). The stability requirement is not that W_t be constant — the architecture exists to let weights move — but that the distribution of outcomes remain inside the envelopes, the velocity limits and the cost budget across the path population. A model family whose behavior bifurcates under modest path variation is treated as non-robust by §23.7 regardless of its mean performance.\n\n§23.4 · LEAKAGE CONTROL\nPurged & Embargoed Validation\nbinding rule [MS §82, COO-24]\nThe binding rule\n“All model calibration, feature construction, optimizer parameter selection and ensemble weighting shall be performed without access to future test-period information. For overlapping data, leakage controls and purging shall be applied where required.” [COO-24]\n\nWalk-forward alone is not airtight when observations overlap: 756-day volatilities, long-window correlations, regime labels and ensemble performance scores can extend into or across the test period, quietly leaking future information into training. Four estimation targets receive the treatment explicitly — volatility, correlations, regimes, model ensemble weights [MS §82] — because their long windows make them the natural leakage channels.\n\nPurging + embargo\n\nPurging removes from the training set any observation whose label window overlaps the test window — a 756-day volatility estimate whose window reaches into the test period is dropped, not truncated:\n\nD_train^purged = { τ : [τ, τ+h_label) ∩ [t, t+h_test) = ∅ }\n\nEmbargo then deletes a further block of training observations immediately following the test window, because serial correlation lets information cross the boundary even without literal overlap:\n\nEmbargo: τ ∈ (t+h_test, t+h_test+h_embargo) excluded\n\nh_embargo is set at least as long as the longest autocorrelation in the return and signal series — by default the 30-day horizon — and is itself a published methodology parameter.\n\nWhy this matters enormously: MTQΣ is intended to govern a monetary reference methodology [COO-24]. A leaked validation record does not merely flatter a trading strategy — it silently changes what is claimed about a unit of account. Every out-of-sample number in the evidence base inherits its credibility from this section, and any methodology version that cannot demonstrate purge and embargo discipline in its validation logs is inadmissible at the gates of §23.12.\n\n§23.7 · SELECTION CRITERION\nThe Model-Selection Criterion\nstability, not return [MS §85]\n\nWhen several candidate models, parameterizations or methodology versions compete, the winner is not the highest return. Return is not the objective of a monetary reference; stability is. The decision function:\n\nScore = a(−Volatility) + b(−CVaR) + c(−MDD) + d(−PPError) + e(−Turnover) + f(−Cost) + g(Diversification) + h(Robustness)\nEVERY TERM ORIENTED SO HIGHER IS MORE STABLE\n\nVolatility, CVaR, maximum drawdown, purchasing-power error, turnover and cost enter negatively; diversification and robustness enter positively. Coefficients a–h are set by the Risk Council within constitutional envelopes, published with the methodology version, and themselves subject to the §23.6 perturbation study — so the ranking of models is not an artifact of the ranking weights.\n\nTWO TERMS DESERVE EMPHASIS\n\nThe drawdown coefficient c is explicitly non-zero because purchasing-power protection is experienced as drawdown, not as variance — a unit whose value never oscillates but occasionally drops 15% has failed its users even if its volatility statistics look excellent (§6.7). The robustness coefficient h aggregates the §23.6 perturbation sensitivities and §23.5 Monte Carlo dispersion, so a model that scores well only on the single realized history — the signature of overfitting — is penalized exactly where it is weakest.\n\nThe criterion operates at three levels: choosing among candidate models inside the ensemble (feeding the adaptive weights of §7.6), choosing the ensemble architecture itself, and promoting a new methodology version through the §23.12 gates. At every level the comparison uses the purged, walk-forward, out-of-sample record — never in-sample statistics, and never a single favorable window.\n\n§23.2 / §23.3 / §23.12 · HONEST STATUS\nRemaining Program vs Live Engine\ngates not executed\nAlready exercised by the reference engine\n✓\nThe S1–S11 deterministic scenario battery — Monte Carlo families, gold and currency stress, redemption runs, correlation collapse and parameter perturbation (§23.5–§23.11) — rendered live in the stress matrix above from /api/validation.\n✓\nCanonical risk state-machine tests — the 6-state machine with hysteresis, throttles and fee ladders.\n✓\nThe canonical invariant suite — constitutional invariants, MARP level logic, chain-link continuity, oracle pause/fallback discipline.\n✓\nDeterministic seeded generators — every scenario re-runs identically, path-for-path (§24.1), so results are reproducible.\nStill required by the full program\n•\nG1 (§23.12) — the 2010-start multi-horizon backtest (§23.2): full pipeline with costs, spanning the 2011 euro crisis, 2013 taper tantrum, 2015 franc depeg, 2020 COVID shock and the 2022–2023 inflation surge.\n•\nG2 (§23.12) — the concatenated walk-forward record (§23.3): expanding and rolling windows, varied re-estimation cadence, under §23.4 purging + embargo with zero leakage findings.\n•\nG4 (§23.12) — Risk-Council robustness bounds B_θ (§23.6): ΔPerformance ≤ B_θ for every registry parameter, not just the perturbation set the reference engine already runs.\n•\nGate execution §23.12 / §25.5: the ordered §23.12 evidence sequence (G1–G7) with independent reproduction, plus the 11 §25.5 production gates — none has been conducted;
