Part 2  Constitutional monetary requirements
The following requirements are non-negotiable.
The developer must not:
1 Peg MTQ  to USD or treat USD as its permanent reference value.
2 Replace the governed currency-and-gold reference basket with a USD peg.
3 Confuse reference-index weights with reserve-portfolio weights.
4 Change MTQ 's purchasing-power definition without explicit governance approval.
5 Allow MASE or MARP to bypass governance, authorization, oracle, reserve-solvency, or execution controls.
6 Introduce a competing rebalancing engine.
7 Treat oracle spot prices as executable route prices.
8 Count the same fee twice or omit an execution cost because of an inconsistent data source.
9 Treat simulated fills as evidence of real execution quality.
10 Treat a reserve ratio as a guarantee of redemption at the reference-index value.
11 Claim that a successful test, audit report, or passage of time automatically authorizes production.
12 Allow the developer to invent objective functions, risk coefficients, threshold policies, price sources, or emergency exceptions.
USD-equivalent values may be used as a common accounting numeraire to compare heterogeneous costs and benefits. That accounting convention does not make MTQ  USD-pegged.
The governed reference index, the reserve portfolio, and the execution system are three different systems:
 Reference index  the governed measurement of MTQ 's purchasing-power reference.
 Reserve portfolio  the actual assets and liabilities, subject to reserve policy, liquidity, market risk, and redemption arrangements.
 Execution system  determines whether a specific proposed transaction is economically justified and operationally safe.
A correct rebalancing algorithm must not silently equate these three concepts.

Part 3  MARP architecture and execution protection
3.1 Separation of responsibilities
MASE calculates the proposed reserve target under the approved methodology.
MARP evaluates whether and how an approved target should be approached through executable transactions.
The architecture must separate these stages:
1 Reference-index calculation.
2 Reserve valuation and holdings reconciliation.
3 Target-weight computation.
4 Trade sizing and direction.
5 Executable route quotation.
6 Estimated cost and adverse-movement protection.
7 Economic eligibility.
8 Authorization and on-chain hard-bound enforcement.
9 Execution.
10 Settlement reconciliation and calibration.
MASE must not execute trades. MARP must not rewrite the governed index. Neither component may bypass the authorization layer.
3.2 Five separate time measurements
Implement and record all five. Do not collapse these into a single "freshness" flag.
1 Oracle observation age  current time minus the oracle observation timestamp.
2 Executable-quote age  current time minus the quote's creation timestamp.
3 Decision-to-submission delay  submission time minus decision time.
4 Submission-to-execution delay  execution/inclusion time minus submission time, where observable.
5 Settlement/finality delay  finality time minus execution/inclusion time.
Each measurement requires a defined clock, units, data source, maximum permitted value, and failure behavior. If a value is not observable or reliable on a particular chain, label it unavailable and apply the approved conservative policy. Do not substitute zero.

3.8 Quote protection and hard execution bounds
Immediately before execution, validate:
 Route quote exists, belongs to the intended assets and trade side, and is fresh.
 Quote timestamp, quote age, expiry, and deadline are valid.
 Oracle data are independently fresh and satisfy approved confidence/deviation checks.
 Input/output amount semantics are correct.
 minAmountOut or maxAmountIn is correctly derived and denominated.
 Route liquidity and size caps are satisfied.
 Fees are correctly represented and not double-counted.
 The proposed trade remains within reserve, turnover, concentration, and authorization limits.
 The keeper is authorized.
 The decision record and approved parameter/model versions are valid.
If the quote is stale, the oracle fails its checks, or the price has deteriorated beyond the hard bound, reject, requote, resize, or defer according to the approved policy.
An on-chain contract cannot independently prove that an off-chain route was globally optimal or that all estimated economic costs were correct. On-chain enforcement must enforce hard bounds and authorization. The keeper performs the approved economic analysis and signs an attributable decision record.
3.9 Emergency mode
Normal, degraded, and emergency modes must be separate, explicitly defined states.
An emergency trade may bypass the normal economic eligibility threshold only if a written policy is approved in advance and defines:
 The triggering conditions.
 The risk-reducing objective.
 Authorized initiators and signers.
 Permitted assets, routes, counterparties, and maximum sizes.
 Maximum frequency and cumulative exposure.
 Required oracle and execution bounds.
 Expiry and automatic return-to-normal behavior.
 Audit logging and post-event review.
 Independent monitoring and kill-switch behavior.
Emergency mode must never implicitly waive authorization, reserve-solvency limits, approved oracle sanity checks, or minimum-output/maximum-input protection.

4.1 Listing C-1  Signed deviation and signed improvement
solidity
// SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT
pragma solidity ^0.8.19;

library DeviationMath {
 error SignedOverflow();

 /// @notice Signed deviation: observed minus target.
 /// Positive means overweight; negative means underweight.
 function signedDeviation(
 int256 observedW,
 int256 targetW
 ) internal pure returns (int256) {
 return observedW - targetW;
 }

 /// @notice Absolute value without accepting int256 minimum.
 function absDeviation(int256 d) internal pure returns (uint256) {
 if (d == type(int256).min) revert SignedOverflow();
 return uint256(d >= 0 ? d : -d);
 }

 /// @notice Signed post-trade deviation.
 /// deltaW is positive for an increase in the asset weight.
 function postTradeDeviation(
 int256 observedW,
 int256 targetW,
 int256 deltaW
 ) internal pure returns (int256) {
 return (observedW + deltaW) - targetW;
 }

 /// @notice Signed improvement: positive improves absolute deviation,
 /// negative worsens, zero means no change.
 function signedImprovement(
 int256 observedW,
 int256 targetW,
 int256 deltaW
 ) internal pure returns (int256) {
 uint256 beforeAbs = absDeviation(signedDeviation(observedW, targetW));
 uint256 afterAbs = absDeviation(postTradeDeviation(observedW, targetW, deltaW));

 if (beforeAbs >= afterAbs) {
 uint256 diff = beforeAbs - afterAbs;
 if (diff > uint256(type(int256).max)) revert SignedOverflow();
 return int256(diff);
 }

 uint256 deterioration = afterAbs - beforeAbs;
 if (deterioration > uint256(type(int256).max)) revert SignedOverflow();
 return -int256(deterioration);
 }

 /// @notice Normal-mode eligibility: positive improvement, correct direction.
 function isEligibleForNormalMode(
 int256 signedImp,
 int256 observedW,
 int256 targetW,
 int256 deltaW
 ) internal pure returns (bool) {
 if (signedImp <= 0) return false;
 int256 d = observedW - targetW;
 if (deltaW > 0 && d >= 0) return false;
 if (deltaW < 0 && d <= 0) return false;
 return true;
 }
}

// SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT
pragma solidity ^0.8.19;

library TokenScale {
 error UnsupportedDecimals(uint8 decimals);
 error PrecisionLoss();
 error ScaleOverflow();

 uint8 internal constant INTERNAL_DECIMALS = 18;

 /// @notice Convert a token-native amount into internal 18-decimal form.
 /// Reverts on decimals > 18 or multiplicative overflow.
 function to18Exact(uint256 amount, uint8 decimals)
 internal pure returns (uint256)
 {
 if (decimals > 18) revert UnsupportedDecimals(decimals);
 uint256 factor = 10 ** uint256(18 - decimals);
 if (amount != 0 && amount > type(uint256).max / factor) {
 revert ScaleOverflow();
 }
 return amount * factor;
 }

 /// @notice Convert internal 18-decimal amount into token-native form.
 /// Reverts if the conversion cannot be represented exactly.
 function from18Exact(uint256 amount18, uint8 decimals)
 internal pure returns (uint256)
 {
 if (decimals > 18) revert UnsupportedDecimals(decimals);
 uint256 factor = 10 ** uint256(18 - decimals);
 if (amount18 % factor != 0) revert PrecisionLoss();
 return amount18 / factor;
 }
}

A.2 Approval and allowance hygiene
Gap in v5.0: v5.0 does not specify how token approvals to the aggregator are managed. The blueprint's mint() and redeem() assume transferFrom works without stating how allowance is set, reset, or scoped.
Required:
 Prefer exact approvals over infinite approvals. When transferring amountIn to the aggregator, approve exactly amountIn, then reset to zero after the swap. If gas cost is prohibitive and a design decision to use infinite approvals is approved, document it and list it as an open decision.
 Handle USDT-style approve. Some ERC-20 tokens (USDT) revert if you change a non-zero allowance to a non-zero allowance. Use OpenZeppelin's SafeERC20.forceApprove (which handles the reset-to-zero case) or a wrapper that does.
 Verify received amounts. After transferFrom, verify the contract's balance increased by exactly amountIn. For fee-on-transfer tokens, this check will fail  which is correct behavior unless the token is specifically approved as fee-on-transfer in the asset registry.
 Audit the aggregator's allowance consumption. After the swap, verify any residual allowance is zero (or reset it explicitly). A residual allowance to a compromised aggregator is a direct theft path.
Recommended pattern:
solidity
import "@openzeppelin/contracts/token/ERC20/utils/SafeERC20.sol";

A.3 Atomicity guarantees for multi-leg swaps
Gap in v5.0: v5.0 says "For a multi-leg route, calculate the compounded conversion and fees for each leg" and "Do not assume that a direct cross-rate is executable" but does not specify what happens if leg 1 succeeds and leg 2 fails.
Required:
For any trade involving more than one leg (e.g., A  USDC  B, or A  USDC, then USDC  B):
 If the swap fits in a single transaction (aggregator supports multi-hop): the whole swap must be atomic. If any leg fails, the entire transaction reverts.
 If the swap requires multiple transactions (aggregator does not support multi-hop for the specific pair, or the chain does not fit it in one block):
 The system must track intermediate state explicitly.
 After leg 1 succeeds, the intermediate asset is not considered reserve value until leg 2 settles. The reserve NAV must reflect this by treating the intermediate as a zero-haircut cash-like position with a documented "in-flight" flag.
 If leg 2 fails, the intermediate position must be either (a) rolled back through a reverse leg within a governed time window, or (b) held as a temporary position with an explicit decision record and a mandatory follow-up deadline.
 The keeper must not submit a second multi-leg trade until the first is fully settled or rolled back.
 On EVM: prefer single-transaction atomic swaps whenever the aggregator supports it. On Monad and Arc, both 1inch-style and 0x-style aggregators support multi-hop in a single calldata payload.
 On Solana: Jupiter supports multi-hop in one transaction, but transaction size limits (1232 bytes) may force splitting. Track intermediate state the same way.

A.4 Dust handling and minimum trade size

 Minimum output amount: Every swap must specify a minAmountOut that is non-zero. Zero-output swaps are rejected.

A.5 Slippage calculation with correct decimals
Gap in v5.0: v5.0's Listing C-2 correctly normalizes internal amounts to 18 decimals, but the slippage calculation in §3.8 and the minAmountOut derivation do not specify what decimals the calculation runs in. Applying 1% slippage to an 18-decimal internal amount and then converting to 6-decimal USDC introduces a rounding error of up to 1e-6 USDC, which is negligible  but applying slippage in the wrong unit can produce a 1e12-scale error.
Required:
The slippage calculation must be performed in the output token's native decimals, not in internal 18-decimal units.
Formula:
text
minAmountOutNative = floor(quotedOutputNative * (1 - tau))


Where:
 quotedOutputNative is the aggregator's quoted output in the output token's native decimals.
 tau is the approved slippage tolerance expressed as a decimal fraction (e.g., 0.01e18 for 1%).
 floor is applied once, after the multiplication.
Do NOT:
 Convert quotedOutputNative to 18-decimal internal units, apply slippage, then convert back. This produces double-rounding.
 Apply slippage as quotedOutputNative * (10000 - tauBps) / 10000 if tauBps is not validated against the token's decimals. A 1 bp slippage on 1,000,000 USDC (6 decimals) is 100 USDC-wei, which is representable  but a 1 bp slippage on a 0-decimal token is 0.01 units, which is not representable and must be rejected or rounded up explicitly.
Recommended implementation:
solidity
function computeMinOut(uint256 quotedOutNative, uint8 outDecimals, uint256 tauE18)
 internal pure returns (uint256)
{
 require(quotedOutNative > 0, "Zero quote output");
 require(tauE18 < 1e18, "Invalid slippage");
 uint256 toleranceNative = quotedOutNative * tauE18 / 1e18;
 uint256 minOutNative = quotedOutNative - toleranceNative;
 require(minOutNative > 0, "Slippage exceeds output");
 return minOutNative;
}


Required test: T-45 (below)  1% slippage on 1,000,000 USDC (6 decimals) gives minOut  990,000 USDC. 1% slippage on 1 PAXG (18 decimals) gives minOut  0.99 PAXG.
Approval: Smart-contract lead + Quant lead.

Part D  Reserve accounting invariants
D.1 Reserve state recomputation after every trade
Gap in v5.0: v5.0 requires reconciliation but does not specify the exact computation and event emission that must follow every trade.
Required:

1 Read settled amounts from the transaction receipt (not from the quote).
2 Recompute holdings: For each asset, newBalance = oldBalance + received - spent.
3 Recompute NAV: NAV = sum(balance_i * price_i * (1 - haircut_i)) using the canonical oracle prices at the settlement block, not the quote-time prices.
4 Recompute RR: RR = NAV / (circulatingSupply * MTQPrice).
5 Recompute LCR: LCR = liquidAssets / stressDemand.
6 Emit event: ReserveStateUpdated(NAV, RR, LCR, block.timestamp, tradeId).
7 Check invariants (see D.2 below).
8 If any invariant is breached: Trigger the appropriate circuit breaker (Warning / Degraded / Halted) per B.1.

pragma solidity ^0.8.19;

library ReserveNAV {
 error InvalidHaircut(uint256 haircut);
 error OracleUnavailable(address asset);

 uint256 constant HAIRCUT_SCALE = 1e18;

 struct Holding {
 address asset;
 uint256 balance; // native decimals
 uint8 decimals;
 uint256 priceE18; // USD price, 1e18 scale
 uint256 haircutE18; // 0.005e18 for 0.5%
 }

 /// @notice Compute NAV in USD-equivalent 1e18 terms.
 function computeNAV(Holding[] memory holdings) internal pure returns (uint256 navE18) {
 for (uint256 i = 0; i < holdings.length; i++) {
 Holding memory h = holdings[i];
 require(h.haircutE18 < HAIRCUT_SCALE, "InvalidHaircut");

 // value = balance × price × (1 - haircut) / (10^decimals × 1e18)
 uint256 balanceE18 = uint256(h.balance) * (10 ** uint256(18 - h.decimals));
 uint256 grossValueE18 = (balanceE18 * h.priceE18) / 1e18;
 uint256 netValueE18 = (grossValueE18 * (HAIRCUT_SCALE - h.haircutE18)) / HAIRCUT_SCALE;
 navE18 += netValueE18;
 }
 }
}

function computeBufferGoldRatio(
 BufferState state,
 uint256 rampStart,
 uint256 rampDuration,
 uint256 rampStartRatio
) internal view returns (uint256) {
 if (state == BufferState.EMERGENCY) return 1e18;
 uint256 target = (state == BufferState.BASE) ? 0.625e18 : 0.85e18;

 uint256 diff = target > rampStartRatio ? target - rampStartRatio : rampStartRatio - target;

}

Part G  MEV / front-running protection
Source: Master Blueprint §12.6 (previously under-specified)Gap: v5.0 mentions "private-mempool or equivalent transaction-submission protections" but does not specify the mechanism, per-chain.
G.1 Why this matters
Every MARP rebalance is a public signal. Without MEV protection:
 Front-running: An attacker sees the pending rebalance and trades ahead of it, moving the price.
 Sandwich attack: An attacker places a buy before and a sell after the rebalance, capturing the price impact.
 Back-running: An attacker extracts value from the post-trade state.
The slippage guard (Extension 1 A.5) limits the loss per trade. But without MEV protection, the attacker simply extracts repeatedly until the slippage guard binds.
G.2 Per-chain MEV protection
Monad Testnet / Monad Production:
 Monad has a different mempool architecture than Ethereum.
 Private submission paths: use Monad's sequencer-based ordering or private RPC if available.
 If unavailable: use a time-locked commit-reveal scheme (see below).
Arc Testnet / Arc Production:
 Arc uses USDC as native gas; mempool architecture TBD by Arc.
 Use private RPC if available; otherwise commit-reveal.
Ethereum Mainnet (if deployed):
 Flashbots Protect RPC or MEV Blocker for private submission.
 Bundle with deadline for atomicity.
Solana Devnet / Mainnet (Gate 7):
 Jito bundles or equivalent private mempool.
 Priority fees to outbid front-runners.
G.3 Fallback when private submission is unavailable
If private submission is unavailable on the target chain, use commit-reveal:
1 Keeper commits keccak256(decision, nonce, salt) on-chain.
2 After a governed delay (e.g., 1 block on fast chains, 12 blocks on Ethereum), keeper reveals decision, nonce, salt.
3 Contract executes only if the reveal matches the commit.
4 Attacker who sees the commit cannot act on it (no information).
5 Attacker who sees the reveal has only the current block to act in  same as a normal transaction.
Cost: Slower execution (2 transactions instead of 1). Acceptable on slow chain; may not be acceptable on fast chain.

Part A  The eight essential gaps
A.1 MASE specification is missing (Gate 2 blocker)
The gap: v5.0, Extension 1, and Extension 2 all treat MASE as a black box. They say "MASE calculates the proposed reserve target." But none of them specify how MASE actually computes weights. The Master Blueprint Chapters 57 do  but the developer is being given v5.0+Ext1+Ext2 as the "single source of truth," and those documents do not include the MASE algorithm.
MARP cannot function without MASE. The direction-aware deviation (v5.0 Listing C-1) needs a target weight targetW. That target comes from MASE. If MASE is not implemented, MARP has nothing to rebalance toward.
Also important: v5.0 §3.5 defines J(s) as the MARP trade-level economic objective. This is not the same as MASE's portfolio-level objective (Master Blueprint §7.4, the 8-term objective). Confusing them will produce a wrong implementation.
What must be specified:
 Read the Master Blueprint Chapters 57 as the MASE specification.
 Understand that v5.0's J(s) (§3.5) is for trade gating, not for MASE optimization.
 MASE's objective (Blueprint §7.4) has 8 terms: Risk, CVaR, PPError, CorrRisk, Turnover, StrategicDrift, Concentration, LiquidityRisk.
 MASE's ensemble (Blueprint §7.5) combines 5+ candidate models (MV, ERC, Max Div, CVaR, PP, Regime, Institutional).
 MASE's adaptive ensemble weights (Blueprint §7.6) use softmax over OOS scores.
Required: OD-31  Confirm that MASE implementation follows Blueprint Chapters 57 with the v5.0 J(s) reserved for MARP trade gating. Confirm the eight-term MASE objective and ensemble architecture are implemented as specified.
Owner: Committee + Smart-contract lead. Blocks: Gate 2.

A.2 Minting and redemption specification is missing (Gate 2 blocker)
The gap: Extension 1 and Extension 2 reference mint() and redeem() in examples, and the reserve needs to be seeded with assets (which requires minting). But neither document specifies the actual mint/redeem formulas, fees, or atomicity. These are in Master Blueprint Chapter 19, which the developer must read.
Without mint/redeem:
 The reserve cannot be seeded with initial assets.
 User deposits cannot be converted to reserve assets.
 Redemptions cannot release reserve assets.
 The token has no economic function.
What must be specified (from Blueprint Chapter 19):
 Mint formula: MTQminted = Xnet / P_MTQ,t where Xnet = X × (1  mintFee) and mintFee = 0.10% (Normal state).
 Redeem formula: RedeemValueUSD = Y × NAVt where NAVt = Vnet,t / Scirc,t; then RedeemValueNet = RedeemValueUSD × (1  redeemFee) with redeemFee = 0.15% (Normal state).
 Slippage guard: minMTQOut = Xnet / P_MTQ,t × (1  user) where user = 0.5% default.
 Single-asset router: Optional; the default is full-basket redemption.
 Atomicity: The entire mint/redeem is one transaction; any failure reverts the whole operation.
 State-dependent fees: The fee schedule depends on the risk state per v5.0's state machine (NORMAL, CAUTION, STRESS, DEFENSIVE, EMERGENCY, RECOVERY). v5.0 does not name this dependency; Extension 3 does.
 Minimum amounts: MIN_MINT = MIN_REDEEM = 1 MTQ (BluePrint §19.6.4).
 Full-basket redemption default: The user receives all fiat exposures plus gold in current reserve proportions, not a single currency.
Required: OD-32  Confirm mint/redeem implementation follows Blueprint Chapter 19 with fee schedule tied to the v5.0 risk state machine.
Owner: Committee + Smart-contract lead. Blocks: Gate 2.

 Disclosure timing is undefined.
 Bounty structure is undefined.
 Legal protections for good-faith researchers are undefined.
This is standard practice for any deployed financial protocol. Its absence is a legal and security risk.
What must be specified:

 Response SLA: e.g., acknowledge within 24 hours, triage within 72 hours.

 Safe harbor: clear statement that good-faith research is authorized and protected.
 Bounty scope and amounts: what's in scope (contracts, oracles, keeper, frontend), what's out of scope, bounty structure (Severity × percentage of funds at risk, or fixed amounts).

Without this, Gate 5 should not open. Gate 2 (testnet) can proceed, but the moment the project announces itself publicly, the policy must exist.

Owner: Security lead + Legal. Blocks: Gate 5 (not Gate 2, but should exist before any public-facing statement).

The four documents (v5.0, Ext 1, Ext 2, Ext 3) together, plus the Master Blueprint v1.0, are a complete specification for a testnet-live single-chain EVM deployment at Gate 2.
The eight items above close the last essential gaps. None of them require new architecture; all of them either:
 Point to an existing Blueprint section that was never formally listed as an input dependency.
 Verify existing infrastructure that was assumed but not checked.
 Add a required policy (vulnerability disclosure) that is standard practice.
What I am not confident about:
I cannot guarantee that no additional gaps exist. What I can say is that any remaining gap would be in one of these categories:
 Deferred by design (portfolio optimization, multichain, yield strategy, Solana)  these are Gate 5 or Gate 7 items.
 Operational (monitoring dashboards, incident response templates, on-call rotation)  these are Governance deliverables, not developer blockers.
 Legal (regulatory classification, KYC/AML, jurisdiction selection)  these are Legal deliverables, not developer blockers.
 External (audit findings, market feedback, commercial evidence)  these are Gate 3 or Gate 6 evidence items.
If the developer begins the first sprint with these four documents and the Master Blueprint, and reports back honestly, the Committee will know within 23 weeks whether any additional gaps exist. The first sprint is itself the verification.
What the developer must not do:
 Assume the four documents are sufficient without reading the Master Blueprint.
 Invent MASE, Mint/Redeem, Genesis, or Oracle addresses.
 Skip Safe verification (OD-35) or Repository access (OD-36).
 Deploy to testnet without running T-01 through T-88 (or documented subset).
 Claim Gate 2 closure without the Layer 8 external specifications integrated.
The one sentence to carry away:
The four documents plus the Master Blueprint are the complete implementation input for Gate 2; the eight items above are the last things a developer must obtain, verify, or confirm before that input is usable.
