 16570→22.1 Constitutional Separation of the Algorithmic Layers
 16571→
 16572→  The architecture has three independent mathematical layers, and their
 16573→  independence is constitutional: each layer is governed, validated and
 16574→  audited separately, so that no single algorithm can silently control
 16575→  the system [MS §90].
 16576→
 16577→  This prevents one algorithm from silently controlling everything.
 16578→
 16579→Layer 1 is the Constituency Engine, and it answers one question: who is
 16580→eligible? (Chapter 4). Its domain is the candidate universe — which
 16581→currencies and gold qualify for the active basket — decided by the
 16582→eligibility score Q_i with entry and exit hysteresis. Its boundary is
 16583→equally narrow: it may never assign a weight, execute a trade or hold an
 16584→asset; it only admits and retires members. Layer 2 is MASE, the
 16585→Multi-model Adaptive Stability Engine, and it answers the question: how
 16586→much weight? (Chapters 5–7). Its domain is the target weight vector for
 16587→the eligible members, produced by the ensemble under the composite
 16588→objective. Its boundary: it weighs only what Layer 1 has admitted, and
 16589→it never executes anything — its output is a target, not a trade. Layer
 16590→3 is the MARP / Reserve Engine, and it answers the question: when and
 16591→how do we execute? (Chapters 10–16). Its domain is execution decisions
 16592→and reserve management. Its boundary: it moves toward Layer 2's targets,
 16593→within Layer 1's membership, under the reserve rules — it never
 16594→redefines the target or the membership. The summary table of Section 2.2
 16595→carries the same three layers with their primary chapters.

 16633→Independent governance means each layer has its own parameter classes in
 16634→the parameter registry (Section 22.4), its own version stream (Section
 16635→24.2) and its own validation tests in the research program (Chapter 23).
 16636→Independent audit means each layer's outputs are reproducible from its
 16637→own published inputs — an auditor can attribute any change in the live
 16638→weight vector to exactly one layer (Section 2.2). The separation is also
 16639→the containment strategy: a faulty optimizer cannot admit a broken
 16640→currency, because Layer 1's eligibility gate stands in front of it; a
 16641→faulty executor cannot chase a bad target, because MARP's justification
 16642→gates (Chapter 10) stand between target and trade; and a compromised
 16643→data feed cannot silently reweight the basket, because the oracle layer
 16644→(Chapter 17) and the registry's envelope checks (Section 7.7) bound what
 16645→any single input can do.

 16647→22.2 Governance Separation
 16648→
 16649→Governance in MTQ Σ approves rules, never outcomes [MS §91]. The
 16650→governance bodies may approve six categories of change:
 16651→
 16652→-   Methodology — the published, versioned methodology that defines how
 16653→    weights are computed (Invariant I3); methodology changes are
 16654→    constitutional and follow the 90-day process.
 16655→
 16656→-   Parameter envelopes — the admissible bounds within which tunable
 16657→    parameters may move (Section 22.4); the envelope itself is
 16658→    constitutional, while the value inside it follows the layer's own
 16659→    path.
 16660→
 16661→-   Eligible data providers — which institutional and oracle sources may
 16662→    feed the engines (Chapters 4 and 17).
 16663→
 16664→-   Eligible reserve instruments — which tokens and instruments may back
 16665→    the unit (Chapter 15).
 16666→
 16667→-   Risk thresholds — the solvency, liquidity and eject thresholds of
 16668→    Chapter 21.
 16669→
 16670→-   Emergency rules — the emergency action set and its authorization
 16671→    (Sections 22.5–22.6).
 16672→
 16673→What no governance body may do — on any layer, under any quorum, in any
 16674→state — is manually set an individual live weight on a discretionary
 16675→basis [MS §91]:
 16676→
 16677→Governance → {rules} Governance not→ W_(USD,t), W_(Gold,t)
 16678→
 16679→This is Invariant I8 (Section 2.6): no governance body may set
 16680→individual live weights by discretion. The prohibition is structural,
 16681→not merely legalistic. Live weights enter the system through exactly one
 16682→door — the MASE submission path of Section 7.7 — which verifies the data
 16683→version, the methodology hash, the constitutional envelopes, the
 16684→velocity limits and the smoothing rule before a vector can become live.
 16685→To set a weight by hand, a governance body would have to counterfeit an
 16686→entire data-and-methodology pipeline, in public, against a contract that
 16687→checks every property it can verify on-chain. Discretionary weights
 16688→would also destroy the two properties the unit is built on:
 16689→reproducibility (Section 24.1 — an auditor could no longer re-derive the
 16690→live vector from published inputs) and the fixed-methodology promise
 16691→itself (Invariant I3).
 16692→
 16693→The boundary has a precise shape: governance can change the rules of the
 16694→game — the methodology, the envelopes, the thresholds — but never the
 16695→score of a particular game. A Risk Council that dislikes the live CHF
 16696→weight may tighten the CHF envelope (a constitutional action),
 16697→commission a methodology revision (also constitutional), or wait for the
 16698→data to move; it may not type a number into the registry and make it
 16699→live. This is what distinguishes an algorithmic monetary architecture
 16700→from a managed one, and it is the reason Invariants I7 and I8 point at
 16701→Sections 22.3 and 22.4 as their enforcement.

 16762→both authorities. Every action on every layer emits events and is
 16763→published (Chapter 24); the layer boundaries are exactly what Invariants
 16764→I7 and I8 reference from Section 2.6.

 16766→22.4 The Parameter Registry and Authority Matrix
 16767→
 16768→Every tunable parameter in the protocol is registered — with its
 16769→identifier, its governance layer, its current value and its admissible
 16770→envelope — in a single on-chain parameter registry [BP §14.4.2]. The
 16771→registry is the normative answer to the question of who may change what:
 16772→an update path exists if and only if the parameter is registered, and
 16773→the layer of the record determines which body, which quorum and which
 16774→timelock govern the change. Unregistered parameters fail closed — the
 16775→governance contract of Listing 14 reverts on any update to an unknown
 16776→parameter identifier — so new parameters must be deliberately registered
 16777→(a constitutional action) before they can ever be touched.
 16778→
 16779→-   Registration declares the parameter identifier, its layer
 16780→    (Constitutional, Monetary, Risk or Emergency), its initial value and
 16781→    its envelope [min, max]; registration and envelope changes follow
 16782→    the constitutional process with the 90-day timelock.
 16783→
 16784→-   Envelope discipline: proposed values must lie inside the registered
 16785→    envelope at proposal time, and are re-checked at execution time and
 16786→    again on arrival at the operations contract — three independent
 16787→    checks, so a stale or manipulated proposal cannot bypass the bounds.
 16788→
 16789→-   Timelock discipline: monetary changes execute only after 48 hours,
 16790→    risk changes only after 24 hours, and the Risk Council may veto a
 16791→    monetary proposal inside its timelock window (Section 22.6).
 16792→
 16793→-   Audit trail: every registration, proposal, execution and veto emits
 16794→    an event and is versioned with the data and methodology versions
 16795→    (Section 24.2), so the complete parameter history is reconstructable
 16796→    after the fact.

 16924→22.5 Emergency Actions
 16925→
 16926→The Emergency layer holds the protocol's fastest powers, and every one
 16927→of them is a verb — pause, force, eject, resume — rather than a setting
 16928→[BP §14.5]. Emergency authority can never change a parameter, never set
 16929→a weight and never amend the constitution; it can only halt or force the
 16930→operations the state machine would otherwise run:

 16937→                                         Council decision  
 16938→
 16939→  pauseRedeeming()     Stops all         EMERGENCY state + Council (4/7)
 16940→                       redemptions       Council vote      
 16941→                       (extreme cases                      
 16942→                       only)

 16945→                       immediate                           
 16946→                       rebalance

 16949→                       ejects a          confirmed         
 16950→                       de-pegged asset                     
 16951→

 16953→                       operations        confirmed

 16955→
 16956→The trigger column shows the two-speed design. The first pause — minting
 16957→at RR < 1.05 — is automatic: the state machine applies it the moment
 16958→STRESS is entered (Section 21.4), so the protocol's first defensive move
 16959→does not wait for humans. The remaining actions are council-triggered,
 16960→because they trade user-facing continuity against risk in ways that
 16961→require judgment. Pausing redemptions is the most drastic user-facing
 16962→action in the system and requires the EMERGENCY state plus a council
 16963→vote. A forced rebalance is a discretionary override of the MARP
 16964→doctrine's justification gates and corresponds to Level 5 of the
 16965→execution hierarchy (Section 10.3). The emergency eject bypasses the
 16966→staged liquidation ladder and executes through emergencyLiquidate
 16967→(Section 21.6) when an issuer freeze is confirmed. And resumeOperations
 16968→is deliberately blocked while the state machine is still in EMERGENCY:
 16969→recovery, not decree, must end an existential state.
 16970→
 16971→Checks and balances. Every emergency action requires the 4/7 Emergency
 16972→Council Multi-Sig with hardware wallet requirements, emits an on-chain
 16973→event, and is reported through the transparency layer (Chapter 24) and
 16974→the honest-status declaration (Section 25). The oracle layer's circuit
 16975→breakers (Chapter 17) engage automatically in EMERGENCY, so price-trust
 16976→failures and solvency failures brake together. The state machine
 16977→continues to run underneath the council's actions: an emergency pause
 16978→does not freeze RR/LCR reporting, state transitions or the recovery
 16979→clock, so the council cannot use a pause to obscure the condition it is
 16980→pausing about. Emergency power, in short, is instant but narrow,
 16981→recorded, and reversible only by recovery — the exact trade the
 16982→four-layer hierarchy of Section 22.3 is designed to force.
