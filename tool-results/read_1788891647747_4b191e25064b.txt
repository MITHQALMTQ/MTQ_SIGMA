    33→## The 4 P0 Findings (Critical, must fix before any mainnet deployment)
    34→
    35→### P0-1 — Structural short-gold exposure (chain-linking defect)
    36→**Found by:** AUDIT-C (stress tests, S5), confirmed by AUDIT-B (C2) and AUDIT-A (P1-3).
    37→
    38→The blueprint §9.2 specifies the chain-linked recursive index:
    39→```
    40→I_(t) = I_(t-1) × Σ_i W_(i,t-1) × (P_(i,t) / P_(i,t-1))
    41→```
    42→This makes each component's contribution to the index return equal to its weight × its price relative. Gold at 26% weight contributes 26% of the index's return — matching the 26% gold in the reserve.
    43→
    44→The engine (`engine.ts::computeGfbIndex`, lines 347–361) and the contract (`MTQSigmaV2.sol::getGFB`, lines 420–431) instead use a **fixed-base Laspeyres aggregate**:
    45→```
    46→GFB_t = Σ_i W^Prior_i × P_(i,t) / GFB_BASE_DENOMINATOR
    47→```
    48→where `GFB_BASE_DENOMINATOR` is computed once in the constructor and never updated. This means gold at $2,500/oz contributes **0.26 × 2500 = $650** to the numerator (out of ~$655 total), so gold's *effective* index weight is **~99.9% by USD notional**, not 26%.
    49→
    50→**The consequence (S5 stress test, 0% survival):**
    51→A +50% gold shock causes:
    52→- The reserve (26% gold, 74% fiat): NAV grows by 0.26 × 50% = **+13%**.
    53→- The liability (P_MTQ = GFB): the GFB index grows by 0.999 × 50% = **+50%**.
    54→- RR = 1.10 × (1.13 / 1.50) = **0.83** — below the 1.00 hard floor.
    55→- Protocol enters EMERGENCY; redemption fee raised to 2%; further redemptions accelerate the spiral; **death spiral in 100% of runs**.
    56→
    57→**This is the single most critical finding in the audit.** It is a math error, not a security bug, but its economic consequence is total protocol failure under the exact stress the protocol was designed to withstand (gold is supposed to be the *crisis diversifier*, not the *crisis trigger*).
    58→
    59→**Fix:** Implement the §9.2 recursive chain-linked formula. Store `I_t` and `W_{i,t-1}` in contract storage; advance `I_t` on every accepted weight commit; rebase the price relatives against `P_{i,t-1}` (the price at the last weight commit). Re-run S5; target ≥ 95% survival.

    61→### P0-2 — Redemption priced against P_MTQ, not NAV_t (Invariant I6 violation)
    62→**Found by:** AUDIT-A (P0-1), confirmed by AUDIT-D (F-EMERG-01 context).
    63→
    64→The blueprint §2.6 Invariant I6 (REVISED v1.0) states: "Redemption is priced against NAV_t, never against a fixed USD peg." §19.1 repeats: "RedeemValue = Y × NAV_(t)". §19.3.2 Step 2 confirms: "RedeemValue_(USD) = Y × NAV_(t)".
    65→
    66→The engine (`engine.ts:933 applyRedeem`) and the contract (`MTQSigmaV2.sol::redeem`, lines 654–682) price redemption as:
    67→```
    68→grossUsd = mtqAmount × P_MTQ   (not Y × NAV_t)
    69→```
    70→where `P_MTQ = GFB_t` (the adaptive index), not `NAV_t = V_net / S_circ`.
    71→
    72→**The contradiction inside the blueprint:** §3.4.2 (legacy v1.2 text retained in the v1.0 doc) says redemption is priced against the GFB index, which the engine follows. §19.x (v1.0 text) says NAV_t. These are different numbers: at the current state (RR = 1.10, P_MTQ = 1.76), NAV_t = 1.10 × 1.76 = **$1.94/MTQ** (10% premium over P_MTQ). Under NAV pricing, redeeming 1 MTQ returns $1.94; under GFB pricing it returns $1.76.
    73→
    74→**Why the engine chose P_MTQ:** At high RR (2400% at genesis with no circulating), NAV_t = 24 × P_MTQ = $42/MTQ. Redeeming 1 MTQ for $42 would drain the reserve. The §3.4.2 (P_MTQ) path is the *arbitrage-safe* one.
    75→
    76→**Verdict:** This is a **blueprint-level contradiction**, not an implementation bug. The v1.0 blueprint text is internally inconsistent (§3.4.2 vs §19.x). The implementation chose the safer interpretation (§3.4.2). **Recommendation:** the blueprint must be reconciled — either restate I6 as "Redemption is priced against the GFB index (P_MTQ) — the arbitrage-safe price — never against NAV_t" (and document why NAV_t is unsafe at high RR), OR define NAV_t as `min(P_MTQ, V_net/S_circ)` and update the implementation. **The current implementation is defensible but the blueprint text is wrong.**

    78→### P0-3 — Only 5 risk states implemented; S3 STRESS (1.02 ≤ RR < 1.05) missing
    79→**Found by:** AUDIT-A (P0-2), confirmed by reading `blueprint.ts:182-190 RISK_STATE_MACHINE` (5 entries) and `MTQSigmaV2.sol::Status` enum (5 values).
    80→
    81→The blueprint §21.2 specifies **SIX** risk states: S1 NORMAL, S2 CAUTION, S3 STRESS, S4 DEFENSIVE, S5 EMERGENCY, S6 RECOVERY. The engine and the contract have **FIVE**: NORMAL, CAUTION, DEFENSIVE, EMERGENCY, RECOVERY. The S3 STRESS state (1.02 ≤ RR < 1.05) is **collapsed into DEFENSIVE** — meaning the protocol skips the "significant stress: minting paused, redeem fee 0.50%, emergency rebalancing triggered" tier and jumps directly from CAUTION (1.05–1.10) to DEFENSIVE (1.00–1.02).
    82→
    83→**Consequence:** The protocol's risk-response granularity is too coarse. Under a moderate shock (RR drops from 1.10 to 1.04), the protocol enters DEFENSIVE (redeem fee 1.00%) when §21.2 says it should enter STRESS (redeem fee 0.50%). This is a 2× fee overcharge on redemers under moderate stress.
    84→
    85→**Fix:** Add S3 STRESS to the `Status` enum (contract) and the `RISK_STATE_MACHINE` constant (TS). Update `determineStatus` to use the 6-state thresholds. Update the redeem fee schedule to include the STRESS tier (0.50%).

    87→### P0-4 — DAO governance has 1 of 4 layers (only Monetary 48h timelock)
    88→**Found by:** AUDIT-B (C3), confirmed by AUDIT-A.
    89→
    90→The blueprint §22.3 specifies **FOUR governance layers**:
    91→1. **Constitutional** — 7/7 Multi-Sig, 90-day timelock — for immutable parameters (architecture, envelopes, hard floors, liquidation staging).
    92→2. **Monetary** — DAO Vote, 51% quorum, 48-hour timelock — for RR target, fee structure, smoothing parameters.
    93→3. **Risk** — Risk Council 4/7, 24-hour timelock — for haircuts, thresholds, eject parameters, LCR targets.
    94→4. **Emergency** — Emergency Council 4/7, instant — for pause/eject/rebalance.
    95→
    96→The `MTQSigmaV2.sol` contract implements **ONE timelock** (48h, ADMIN_ROLE), which corresponds to the **Monetary** layer only. There is no 90-day Constitutional timelock, no 24-hour Risk Council timelock, no instant Emergency Council path. The 5 AccessControl roles (ADMIN/MINTER/PAUSER/KEEPER/ORACLE) are placeholders for the 4 governance layers but don't enforce the distinct timelocks or quorum requirements.
    97→
    98→**Consequence:** The contract's `getHonestStatus()` returns `0x7FF` (bit 9 = `daoGovernance` set), but the honest value is **bit 9 = 0** — only the Monetary layer is implemented. Any privileged parameter change (e.g., raising the mint fee from 10bps to 100bps) takes 48h, not the 90-day Constitutional timelock that §22.3 requires for fundamental architecture changes.
    99→
   100→**Fix:** Implement 3 separate timelock controllers: `ConstitutionalTimelock` (90 days, 7/7), `MonetaryTimelock` (48h, 51% DAO — already implemented), `RiskTimelock` (24h, 4/7). Emergency Council can be a multi-sig with instant execution gated by 4/7 signature collection. Until these exist, clear bit 9 in `getHonestStatus()` and re-issue as `0x5A7`.

   180→## Tokenomics Findings (AUDIT-D) — CFO View
   181→
   182→### The RR decay math (the single most important tokenomics finding)
   183→
   184→At genesis: 1,000,000 MTQ locked in genesis reserve (excluded from circulating), 1,100,000 USDC collateral → initial RR = 1.10 (TARGET). As users mint X USDC:
   185→
   186→| X (USDC minted) | V (NAV) | L (Liability) | RR | Status |
   187→|---|---|---|---|---|
   188→| $1M | $2.099M | $0.999M | **2.1011** | NORMAL |
   189→| $5M | $6.095M | $4.995M | **1.2202** | NORMAL |
   190→| $10M | $11.090M | $9.990M | **1.1101** | NORMAL |
   191→| $11M | $12.089M | $10.989M | **1.1001** | ← re-equilibrates at TARGET |
   192→| $25M | $26.075M | $24.975M | **1.0440** | ⚠️ STRESS |
   193→| $50M | $51.050M | $49.950M | **1.0220** | ⚠️ DEFENSIVE |
   194→| $110M | $110.990M | $109.890M | **1.0100** | 🔴 DEFENSIVE |
   195→| $500M | $500.600M | $499.500M | **1.0022** | 🔴 EMERGENCY |
   196→| $1B | $1,000.100M | $999.000M | **1.0011** | 🔴 approaching HARD floor |
   197→
   198→**The protocol's RR naturally decays toward the 1.00 hard floor as minting scales.** The 1,100,000 USDC genesis buffer is finite and amortized over an ever-growing liability. Without ongoing rebalancing + reserve growth (fees, treasury sweep, yield on reserve assets), the protocol asymptotes to insolvency at scale.
   199→
   200→**Mitigation in the blueprint:** §13.2 reserve growth mechanisms + §20.4 fee accrual + §13.4 LCR-driven emergency rebalancing. But the *engine* and the *contract* don't auto-rebalance to grow the reserve — the keeper must do it via `executeRebalance`. If the keeper doesn't act, RR decays.
   201→
   202→**Recommendation:** Document the RR decay curve in the Honest Status. Add an auto-rebalance trigger at RR < 1.05 (STRESS) that the keeper MUST execute within 24h (timelock-gated). At RR < 1.02 (DEFENSIVE), force-execute the rebalance regardless of keeper action.
   203→
   204→### Fee accounting mismatch
   205→
   206→The contract accrues fees to `reserveVault` (which counts toward NAV/RR). The blueprint §20.4 specifies a separate **Operational Wallet** for fees (to pay gas, oracles, keepers). Without this separation, fees inflate reported RR by 10–25 bps (the fees look like reserve growth but are actually operational budget). This is a **medium-high tokenomics finding** because it makes the protocol look better-capitalized than it is.
   207→
   208→### EMERGENCY redemption — blueprint self-contradiction
   209→
   210→- §16.2 (Buffer States table): "EMERGENCY — Redemption is still allowed (with increased fee)."
   211→- §21.4 (Action Matrix): "S5 EMERGENCY — Redemption → Paused."
   212→
   213→These contradict. The contract follows §16.2 (redemption still allowed, fee raised to 2%). The blueprint §21.4 text says redemption should be paused in S5. **Recommendation:** the blueprint must be reconciled. The safer interpretation is §21.4 (pause redemption in EMERGENCY) because a 2% fee is unlikely to deter a panic-driven bank run; only a pause stops the bleeding. But pausing redemption breaks the user exit — a philosophical choice the protocol must make explicitly.
   214→
   215→### CHF base-fixing inconsistency
   216→
   217→- Engine and contract: `BASE_CHF_USD = 0.88` (1 CHF = $0.88)
   218→- Blueprint Listing 10 §18.5 + Listing 12 §20.5: `1.13e18` (1 CHF = $1.13)
   219→
   220→CHF is underweighted in the engine's GFB denominator by 28.2% (0.044 vs 0.0565). This makes the GFB base denominator too small, so the GFB index is overstated by ~0.002% (small but real). **Recommendation:** reconcile to the blueprint's $1.13 (or update the blueprint to $0.88 if the engine's value is the intended one) — but document the choice.
