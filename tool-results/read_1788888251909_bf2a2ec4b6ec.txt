 14940→21.1 Purpose
 14941→
 14942→  The risk state machine is the protocol's immune system: a
 14943→  deterministic control plane that continuously diagnoses the solvency
 14944→  and liquidity condition of MTQ Σ and reconfigures the protocol's
 14945→  operating posture in response [BP §14.1]. It is the single layer at
 14946→  which fees, execution behavior, rebalancing urgency, oracle
 14947→  confirmation depth and governance communication all change together —
 14948→  coherently, predictably and without human discretion — as conditions
 14949→  deteriorate and recover.
 14950→
 14951→The module performs four functions [BP §14.1]. First, it defines the
 14952→risk state of the protocol — one of NORMAL, CAUTION, STRESS, DEFENSIVE,
 14953→EMERGENCY or RECOVERY — derived from the Reserve Ratio (RR) and the
 14954→Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), both computed by the reserve valuation
 14955→layer with asset-specific haircuts (Chapter 14; Invariant I4). Second,
 14956→it enforces state-dependent actions: minting throttles, redemption fees,
 14957→rebalancing urgency and communication cadence. Third, it provides the
 14958→governance hierarchy with a clear separation of powers — Constitutional,
 14959→Monetary, Risk and Emergency (Section 22.3). Fourth, it enables
 14960→time-locked parameter updates, so that no parameter can be changed
 14961→maliciously or in a rush (Sections 22.4 and 22.6).
 14962→
 14963→1.  Deterministic state. The state is derived entirely from on-chain
 14964→    metrics (RR, LCR, oracle health); no human subjectivity is involved
 14965→    in the classification.
 14966→
 14967→2.  Monotonic transitions under stress. The protocol moves immediately
 14968→    toward more restrictive states during a crisis, while recovery
 14969→    requires sustained, time-based improvement.
 14970→
 14971→3.  Layered governance. Constitutional parameters are immutable,
 14972→    monetary parameters require a DAO vote and timelock, risk parameters
 14973→    require Risk Council approval, and emergency actions require a
 14974→    Multi-Sig with hardware wallet requirements.
 14975→
 14976→The risk state must not be confused with the crisis score of Section
 14977→5.5. The crisis score aggregates market-side stress evidence (FX
 14978→volatility, cross-asset correlation, liquidity stress, drawdowns) and
 14979→modulates the MASE objective and the execution posture (§13.7); the risk
 14980→state is the protocol-side solvency and liquidity condition of the
 14981→reserve itself. The two are complementary and converge on execution: a
 14982→market crisis without solvency stress changes how carefully the system
 14983→trades, while a deterioration of RR or LCR changes what the system is
 14984→permitted to do at all, including the state-contingent reserve posture
 14985→of Sections 14.4–14.5. Both publish their outputs through the
 14986→transparency layer (Chapter 24), and both feed the honest-status
 14987→declaration of Section 25.

 15048→Two reading rules apply to the table. First, both metrics must qualify
 15049→for a state: NORMAL requires RR ≥ 1.10 and LCR ≥ 1.00 simultaneously, so

 15054→(Section 2.6), which is why the S5 entry trigger is immediate and why

 15056→22.4).

 15062→1.05 with LCR below 0.70 — or during a confirmed climb back from a
 15063→deeper state. Its action profile (25% minting throttle, 0.30% redemption
 15064→fee, increased rebalancing urgency) sits deliberately between CAUTION
 15065→and STRESS, which is also how the transition logic of Section 21.3 ranks
 15066→it.

 15068→21.3 State Transition Logic
 15069→
 15070→State transitions obey one asymmetric rule [BP §14.2.2]. Movement toward
 15071→a more restrictive state (for example NORMAL → CAUTION) is immediate
 15072→upon breaching the threshold. Movement toward a less restrictive state
 15073→(for example STRESS → CAUTION) requires the protocol to remain above the
 15074→destination state's threshold continuously for 48 consecutive hours —
 15075→the recovery confirmation period. Degradation is fast; recovery is slow
 15076→and must be earned:
 15077→
 15078→NORMAL → CAUTION → STRESS → DEFENSIVE → EMERGENCY (immediate on breach)
 15079→
 15080→EMERGENCY → DEFENSIVE → STRESS → CAUTION → NORMAL (48 h sustained
 15081→confirmation per step)
 15082→
 15083→t_(confirm) ≥ 48 h (sustained, no breach)
 15084→
 15085→Each upward step requires the metrics to satisfy the destination state's
 15086→thresholds continuously for the confirmation period: 48 hours at RR ≥
 15087→1.10 to leave CAUTION into NORMAL, 48 hours at RR ≥ 1.05 to leave
 15088→STRESS, 48 hours at RR ≥ 1.02 to leave DEFENSIVE, and 48 hours at RR ≥
 15089→1.00 to leave EMERGENCY. The confirmation period is the control-plane
 15090→member of the protocol's anti-oscillation family — it composes with
 15091→constituency hysteresis (Section 4.7), stress-adaptive smoothing
 15092→(Section 8.4) and the rebalancing direction lock (Section 11.5), each of
 15093→which prevents oscillation at a different timescale. A noisy metric
 15094→sequence can therefore never flip the protocol between postures; the
 15095→worst it can do is hold the protocol in the more conservative state.
 15096→
 15097→The 48-hour constant is carried in the reference implementation as
 15098→RECOVERY_CONFIRMATION_PERIOD (Listing 13), alongside the immutable
 15099→RR_HARD_FLOOR of Invariant I2. The source parameter table classes the
 15100→confirmation period as a Monetary (DAO 51%) parameter while the
 15101→reference contract declares it immutable; this edition retains the
 15102→immutable constant as the conservative reading — a stricter governance
 15103→class than the table assigns — and flags the discrepancy for resolution
 15104→during deployment registration (Section 22.4).
 15105→
 15106→Operationally, updateState() runs after every mint, redeem, rebalance
 15107→and price update (the _updateState hook of Listing 13), so the state is
 15108→never stale by more than one state-relevant event. Because deterioration
 15109→is immediate and recovery is delayed, the machine is biased toward
 15110→conservatism by construction — exactly the bias an instrument whose
 15111→promise is purchasing-power protection should have.

 15174→21.5 Geopolitical Eject — Staged Liquidation and Reintegration
 15175→
 15176→  The Geopolitical Eject module is the reserve-level emergency defence
 15177→  of the protocol: it protects the assets backing MTQ Σ from instruments
 15178→  that become unstable, de-pegged, frozen or sanctioned [BP §11.1].
 15179→  Where the risk state machine guards the protocol's solvency ratios,
 15180→  the eject module guards the quality of the individual reserve assets
 15181→  themselves.
 15182→
 15183→  When an asset becomes unreliable, exit slowly and intelligently. Never
 15184→  be a forced seller into a thin market.
 15185→
 15186→Unlike a simple sell-everything response, the module uses a staged
 15187→liquidation ladder that minimizes market impact and avoids crystallizing
 15188→losses during temporary dislocations, and a reintegration score that
 15189→prevents gaming on the way back in [BP §11.1]. The module depends on
 15190→three neighbors: the Asset Admission Registry for asset metadata
 15191→(Chapter 15), the oracle architecture for price feeds (Chapter 17), and
 15192→the execution layer for trade execution (Section 11.11). One boundary
 15193→must be stated crisply: liquidation proceeds are redeployed into
 15194→eligible reserve assets under the reserve rules (Chapters 13–15),
 15195→because the eject module protects solvency — it never participates in
 15196→secondary-market price support of the MTQ Σ token itself, which is
 15197→constitutionally forbidden (Invariant I12, Section 13.6).
 15198→
 15199→21.5.1 Purpose
 15200→
 15201→The module exists because reserve instruments — stablecoins above all —
 15202→carry issuer risk that the index layer cannot see. A reference weight of
 15203→5% CNY exposure is a monetary statement; the reserve instrument
 15204→implementing that exposure can still de-peg, freeze redemptions, or fall
 15205→under sanctions. The eject module is the emergency defence mechanism
 15206→that answers instrument-level unreliability: it detects the failure
 15207→(Section 21.5.2), exits it gradually (Section 21.5.3), re-admits it only
 15208→under accumulated evidence (Section 21.5.4), and bypasses all gradualism
 15209→when the issuer itself fails (Section 21.5.5) [BP §11.1].
 15210→
 15211→The design tension the module resolves is time. Selling 100% of a
 15212→distressed position at once converts a temporary dislocation into a
 15213→permanent loss and makes the protocol the forced seller that thin
 15214→markets punish. Selling too slowly converts a deteriorating position
 15215→into a solvency problem. The staged ladder prices that tension
 15216→explicitly: exit velocity increases with the duration of the distress
 15217→and with the deterioration of the reserve ratio, so the protocol sells
 15218→faster precisely when the evidence gets worse — and pauses when its own
 15219→selling starts moving the market against it (the 5% rule, Section
 15220→21.5.3).
 15221→
 15222→21.5.2 The De-peg Detector
 15223→
 15224→The protocol continuously monitors each stablecoin held in the reserve
 15225→against its fiat peg, using the oracle architecture (Chapter 17) [BP
 15226→§11.2]. Monitoring attaches to the currency code of the exposure: the
 15227→specific token implementations are resolved through the Asset Admission
 15228→Registry (Chapter 15), and every admitted instrument for a monitored
 15229→currency is checked independently. Gold is not part of this detector —
 15230→it has no peg; its source risk is handled instead by the canonical
 15231→multi-source price and its independent-source quorum (Chapter 17).
 15232→
 15233→Each stablecoin has an asset-specific peg band [BP §11.2.1]:
 15234→
 15235→  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
 15236→  Asset                 Peg Band (Lower)  Peg Band (Upper)  Rationale
 15237→  --------------------- ----------------- ----------------- -----------------
 15238→  USDC, USDP            0.995             1.005             Extremely liquid;
 15239→                                                            tight band.
 15240→
 15241→  EURC                  0.995             1.005             Highly liquid;
 15242→                                                            tight band.
 15243→
 15244→  GBP                   0.990             1.010             Lower liquidity;
 15245→  (registry-resolved)                                       wider band.
 15246→
 15247→  JPY                   0.990             1.010             Lower liquidity;
 15248→  (registry-resolved)                                       wider band.
 15249→
 15250→  CNY                   0.985             1.015             Limited
 15251→  (registry-resolved,                                       liquidity; widest
 15252→  CNH)                                                      band.
 15253→  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
 15254→
 15255→The band width tracks liquidity: the deepest markets (USDC, USDP, EURC)
 15256→admit a tight ±0.5% band because routine trading noise stays inside it,
 15257→while the less liquid currency implementations admit wider bands so that
 15258→ordinary illiquidity is not mistaken for a broken peg. The detector rule
 15259→is then [BP §11.2.1]:
 15260→
 15261→Unstable ⇔ ( P_(t) < band_(low) or P_(t) > band_(high) ) for more than
 15262→12 consecutive hours
 15263→
 15264→The 12-hour window is deliberately conservative: it prevents false
 15265→positives from temporary wicks and flash dislocations, which routinely
 15266→resolve within hours. The consecutive-hours requirement is equally
 15267→deliberate: the timer resets to zero the moment the price returns inside
 15268→the band, so an asset oscillating in and out of the band never
 15269→accumulates detector time. The detector function (isDepegged, Listing
 15270→13) implements exactly this logic — timer start on the first
 15271→outside-band observation, timer reset on return, flag after the window —
 15272→and the detection window and the bands themselves are Risk-Council
 15273→parameters in the parameter registry (Section 22.4).
 15274→
 15275→21.5.3 The Staged Liquidation Ladder
 15276→
 15277→Once a de-peg is confirmed, the protocol executes a staged liquidation
 15278→rather than selling 100% at once [BP §11.3]. Each stage sells an
 15279→additional fraction of the original position, and each stage's trigger
 15280→conditions escalate with elapsed distress time and with the protocol's
 15281→own reserve ratio:
 15282→
 15283→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
 15284→  Stage             Cumulative Sell % Trigger Condition Action
 15285→  ----------------- ----------------- ----------------- -----------------
 15286→  Stage 1           10%               De-peg confirmed  Sell 10% of the
 15287→                                      for more than 12  asset
 15288→                                      hours             immediately.
 15289→
 15290→  Stage 2           25%               De-peg for more   Sell an
 15291→                                      than 24 hours OR  additional 15%
 15292→                                      RR < 1.08         (total 25%).
 15293→
 15294→  Stage 3           50%               De-peg for more   Sell an
 15295→                                      than 48 hours OR  additional 25%
 15296→                                      RR < 1.05         (total 50%).
 15297→
 15298→  Stage 4           100%              Issuer freeze     Sell the
 15299→                                      confirmed OR RR < remaining 50%
 15300→                                      1.02              (total 100%).
 15301→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
 15302→
 15303→The ladder couples asset-level distress to protocol-level solvency: the
 15304→RR thresholds 1.08, 1.05 and 1.02 sit exactly at the state boundaries of
 15305→Section 21.2, so a de-pegging asset is liquidated faster precisely as
 15306→the protocol itself slides toward STRESS and DEFENSIVE. The cumulative
 15307→percentages (10% → 25% → 50% → 100%) are constitutional and immutable
 15308→(Section 22.4): the shape of the ladder is part of the promise that the
 15309→protocol will never dump a distressed position in one trade, while the
 15310→trigger conditions allow governance to tune sensitivity within that
 15311→fixed shape. One rule guards every stage transition [BP §11.3.1]: each
 15312→stage executes only if the previous stage's sell did not worsen the
 15313→deviation by more than 5%.
 15314→
 15315→The 5% rule formalizes the pause [BP §11.3.3]:
 15316→
 15317→next stage paused ⇔ (P_(stage start) - P_(t))/(P_(stage start)) > 5%
 15318→
 15319→If, during a staged liquidation, the asset price drops more than 5% from
 15320→the price at the start of the current stage, the protocol pauses the
 15321→next stage until the price stabilizes — no further drop for 24 hours.
 15322→The rule exists because the protocol's own selling can become the
 15323→market's problem: if each tranche moves the price against the next, the
 15324→ladder halts rather than chasing the price down. Exiting is thereby
 15325→bounded in both directions — never all at once, and never into a
 15326→collapsing tape. Sell execution itself runs through the execution layer
 15327→(Section 11.11), whose crisis-mode rules widen no-trade thresholds and
 15328→shrink execution sizes precisely when conditions are distressed (Section
 15329→13.7).
 15330→
 15331→21.5.4 The Reintegration Score (Anti-Gaming)
 15332→
 15333→An ejected asset is not automatically re-purchased when it returns to
 15334→its peg band [BP §11.4]. A naive band-triggered re-entry would be
 15335→gameable: an attacker with sufficient market power could push the price
 15336→back inside the band temporarily, trigger re-purchase at distressed
 15337→prices, and profit as the peg breaks again. The reintegration score
 15338→closes that attack surface by demanding sustained, multi-dimensional
 15339→evidence of genuine stability:
 15340→
 15341→  R_(score) = w₁ · TimeInBand + w₂ · LiquidityDepth + w₃ ·
 15342→  OracleAgreement + w₄ · (1 - Volatility)
 15343→
 15344→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
 15345→  Component               Weight (w_i)            Description
 15346→  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
 15347→  TimeInBand              0.40                    Duration the asset has
 15348→                                                  traded within its peg
 15349→                                                  band (scaled 0–1;
 15350→                                                  maximum 7 days).
 15351→
 15352→  LiquidityDepth          0.25                    24-hour DEX volume
 15353→                                                  relative to the
 15354→                                                  protocol's holdings
 15355→                                                  (scaled 0–1).
 15356→
 15357→  OracleAgreement         0.20                    Agreement between the
 15358→                                                  three oracle sources
 15359→                                                  (scaled 0–1).
 15360→
 15361→  Volatility              0.15                    24-hour price
 15362→                                                  volatility (scaled 0–1,
 15363→                                                  higher = more volatile;
 15364→                                                  enters the score as 1 −
 15365→                                                  Volatility, so calm
 15366→                                                  markets score higher).
 15367→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
 15368→
 15369→Time-in-band dominates (w_1 = 0.40) because persistence is the strongest
 15370→single evidence that a peg has genuinely healed; liquidity depth guards
 15371→against re-entering a market too thin to absorb the protocol's size;
 15372→oracle agreement (Chapter 17) requires the independent sources to
 15373→concur, defeating single-feed manipulation; and the volatility term
 15374→penalizes choppy in-band trading, which is stability in appearance only.
 15375→The component weights and the threshold are Risk-Council parameters
 15376→(Section 22.4); the score is evaluated by the re-entry function of
 15377→Listing 13, whose helper functions query oracle history and DEX volume
 15378→data [BP §11]. The re-entry threshold is:
 15379→
 15380→R_(score) ≥ 0.80
 15381→
 15382→A score at or above 0.80 means the asset is considered stable enough to
 15383→begin re-entry — but re-entry itself is staged, mirroring the
 15384→liquidation ladder in reverse [BP §11.4.3]:
 15385→
 15386→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
 15387→  Stage                   Cumulative Buy %        Condition
 15388→  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
 15389→  1                       25%                     R_score ≥ 0.80
 15390→                                                  sustained for 24 hours
 15391→
 15392→  2                       50%                     R_score ≥ 0.85
 15393→                                                  sustained for 48 hours
 15394→
 15395→  3                       100%                    R_score ≥ 0.90
 15396→                                                  sustained for 72 hours
 15397→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
 15398→
 15399→The reverse ladder is deliberately stricter than the exit ladder at
 15400→every step: it requires higher scores (0.80, 0.85, 0.90) sustained for
 15401→longer (24, 48, 72 hours) as the position size grows. The asymmetry is
 15402→the anti-gaming posture of the whole module — getting out is defensive
 15403→and starts small; getting back in is offensive and starts only under
 15404→accumulated evidence. Re-purchased amounts are executed through the same
 15405→execution layer (Section 11.11) and remain subject to the admission
 15406→rules of Chapter 15, so a fully reintegrated asset ends where it
 15407→started: an admitted, monitored reserve instrument.
 15408→
 15409→21.5.5 Special Cases — Issuer Freeze and Sanctions
 15410→
 15411→If an asset issuer freezes redemptions or is sanctioned, the protocol
 15412→bypasses the staged ladder entirely and executes immediate 100%
 15413→liquidation [BP §11.5]. The logic is that a freeze or sanctions event is
 15414→not a price dislocation that may heal — it is a structural impairment of
 15415→the instrument's redeemability and legality, and every hour of continued
 15416→holding concentrates legal and solvency risk. The staged ladder's
 15417→virtue, patience, would here be its vice.
 15418→
 15419→Freeze detection runs on two channels [BP §11.5.1]. On-chain, the
 15420→protocol watches for the asset's pause() or freeze() functions being
 15421→called, detected via event logs. Off-chain, an issuer announcement or
 15422→regulator action is reported through the governance oracle (Chapter 17)
 15423→and confirmed by the Risk Council. When either channel confirms, the
 15424→emergencyLiquidate function (Listing 13) sells the entire balance and
 15425→removes the asset from the Asset Admission Registry.
 15426→
 15427→Two safeguards discipline this strongest of powers. The function is
 15428→executable only by the Emergency Council — a 4/7 Multi-Sig with hardware
 15429→wallet requirements [BP §11] — and removal from the registry means that
 15430→re-admission can never occur through the reintegration score alone: a
 15431→frozen-then-unfrozen or delisted-then-relisted instrument must pass the
 15432→full admission process of Chapter 15 again, including eligibility,
 15433→custody and issuer-quality review. The special case is thus the one
 15434→place the protocol moves fast, and it pays for that speed with the
 15435→slowest possible path back in.
 15436→
 15437→21.6 Smart Contract Implementation — Risk State Machine and Eject
 15438→
 15439→The state machine and the eject module deploy as a single operations
 15440→contract: the states share the reserve-ratio feeds, the eject ladder's
