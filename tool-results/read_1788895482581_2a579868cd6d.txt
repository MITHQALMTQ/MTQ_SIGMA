     3→ 16570→22.1 Constitutional Separation of the Algorithmic Layers
     4→ 16571→
     5→ 16572→  The architecture has three independent mathematical layers, and their
     6→ 16573→  independence is constitutional: each layer is governed, validated and
     7→ 16574→  audited separately, so that no single algorithm can silently control
     8→ 16575→  the system [MS §90].
     9→ 16576→
    10→ 16577→  This prevents one algorithm from silently controlling everything.
    11→ 16578→
    12→ 16579→Layer 1 is the Constituency Engine, and it answers one question: who is
    13→ 16580→eligible? (Chapter 4). Its domain is the candidate universe — which
    14→ 16581→currencies and gold qualify for the active basket — decided by the
    15→ 16582→eligibility score Q_i with entry and exit hysteresis. Its boundary is
    16→ 16583→equally narrow: it may never assign a weight, execute a trade or hold an
    17→ 16584→asset; it only admits and retires members. Layer 2 is MASE, the
    18→ 16585→Multi-model Adaptive Stability Engine, and it answers the question: how
    19→ 16586→much weight? (Chapters 5–7). Its domain is the target weight vector for
    20→ 16587→the eligible members, produced by the ensemble under the composite
    21→ 16588→objective. Its boundary: it weighs only what Layer 1 has admitted, and
    22→ 16589→it never executes anything — its output is a target, not a trade. Layer
    23→ 16590→3 is the MARP / Reserve Engine, and it answers the question: when and
    24→ 16591→how do we execute? (Chapters 10–16). Its domain is execution decisions
    25→ 16592→and reserve management. Its boundary: it moves toward Layer 2's targets,
    26→ 16593→within Layer 1's membership, under the reserve rules — it never
    27→ 16594→redefines the target or the membership. The summary table of Section 2.2
    28→ 16595→carries the same three layers with their primary chapters.

    65→ 16632→
    66→ 16633→Independent governance means each layer has its own parameter classes in
    67→ 16634→the parameter registry (Section 22.4), its own version stream (Section
    68→ 16635→24.2) and its own validation tests in the research program (Chapter 23).
    69→ 16636→Independent audit means each layer's outputs are reproducible from its
    70→ 16637→own published inputs — an auditor can attribute any change in the live
    71→ 16638→weight vector to exactly one layer (Section 2.2). The separation is also
    72→ 16639→the containment strategy: a faulty optimizer cannot admit a broken
    73→ 16640→currency, because Layer 1's eligibility gate stands in front of it; a
    74→ 16641→faulty executor cannot chase a bad target, because MARP's justification
    75→ 16642→gates (Chapter 10) stand between target and trade; and a compromised
    76→ 16643→data feed cannot silently reweight the basket, because the oracle layer
    77→ 16644→(Chapter 17) and the registry's envelope checks (Section 7.7) bound what
    78→ 16645→any single input can do.
    79→ 16646→
    80→ 16647→22.2 Governance Separation
    81→ 16648→
    82→ 16649→Governance in MTQ Σ approves rules, never outcomes [MS §91]. The
    83→ 16650→governance bodies may approve six categories of change:
    84→ 16651→
    85→ 16652→-   Methodology — the published, versioned methodology that defines how
    86→ 16653→    weights are computed (Invariant I3); methodology changes are
    87→ 16654→    constitutional and follow the 90-day process.
    88→ 16655→
    89→ 16656→-   Parameter envelopes — the admissible bounds within which tunable
    90→ 16657→    parameters may move (Section 22.4); the envelope itself is
    91→ 16658→    constitutional, while the value inside it follows the layer's own
    92→ 16659→    path.
    93→ 16660→
    94→ 16661→-   Eligible data providers — which institutional and oracle sources may
    95→ 16662→    feed the engines (Chapters 4 and 17).
    96→ 16663→
    97→ 16664→-   Eligible reserve instruments — which tokens and instruments may back
    98→ 16665→    the unit (Chapter 15).
    99→ 16666→
   100→ 16667→-   Risk thresholds — the solvency, liquidity and eject thresholds of
   101→ 16668→    Chapter 21.
   102→ 16669→
   103→ 16670→-   Emergency rules — the emergency action set and its authorization
   104→ 16671→    (Sections 22.5–22.6).
   105→ 16672→
   106→ 16673→What no governance body may do — on any layer, under any quorum, in any
   107→ 16674→state — is manually set an individual live weight on a discretionary
   108→ 16675→basis [MS §91]:
   109→ 16676→
   110→ 16677→Governance → {rules} Governance not→ W_(USD,t), W_(Gold,t)
   111→ 16678→
   112→ 16679→This is Invariant I8 (Section 2.6): no governance body may set
   113→ 16680→individual live weights by discretion. The prohibition is structural,
   114→ 16681→not merely legalistic. Live weights enter the system through exactly one
   115→ 16682→door — the MASE submission path of Section 7.7 — which verifies the data
   116→ 16683→version, the methodology hash, the constitutional envelopes, the
   117→ 16684→velocity limits and the smoothing rule before a vector can become live.
   118→ 16685→To set a weight by hand, a governance body would have to counterfeit an
   119→ 16686→entire data-and-methodology pipeline, in public, against a contract that
   120→ 16687→checks every property it can verify on-chain. Discretionary weights
   121→ 16688→would also destroy the two properties the unit is built on:
   122→ 16689→reproducibility (Section 24.1 — an auditor could no longer re-derive the
   123→ 16690→live vector from published inputs) and the fixed-methodology promise
   124→ 16691→itself (Invariant I3).
   125→ 16692→
   126→ 16693→The boundary has a precise shape: governance can change the rules of the
   127→ 16694→game — the methodology, the envelopes, the thresholds — but never the
   128→ 16695→score of a particular game. A Risk Council that dislikes the live CHF
   129→ 16696→weight may tighten the CHF envelope (a constitutional action),
   130→ 16697→commission a methodology revision (also constitutional), or wait for the
   131→ 16698→data to move; it may not type a number into the registry and make it
   132→ 16699→live. This is what distinguishes an algorithmic monetary architecture
   133→ 16700→from a managed one, and it is the reason Invariants I7 and I8 point at
   134→ 16701→Sections 22.3 and 22.4 as their enforcement.

   174→ 16741→Reading the table: Constitutional authority (a 7/7 Multi-Sig with a
   175→ 16742→90-day timelock) covers the immutable core — the architecture, the
   176→ 16743→constitutional envelopes, the hard floors, the fixed methodology and the
   177→ 16744→liquidation ladder's shape. Monetary authority (DAO vote, 51% quorum,
   178→ 16745→48-hour timelock) covers the monetary-policy parameters: the RR targets,
   179→ 16746→the fee structure and the smoothing parameters. Risk authority (Risk
   180→ 16747→Council, 4/7 with a 24-hour timelock) covers the technical risk
   181→ 16748→parameters: haircuts, thresholds, eject parameters and LCR targets.
   182→ 16749→Emergency authority (Emergency Council, 4/7, instant) covers only the
   183→ 16750→pause/eject/rebalance action set of Section 22.5 — the one layer that
   184→ 16751→trades deliberation for speed, and pays for it with the narrowest scope
   185→ 16752→in the system.
   186→ 16753→
   187→ 16754→The layering implements Invariant I7 (Section 2.6): the protocol is
   188→ 16755→governed by a layered hierarchy, and no layer can reach into another's
   189→ 16756→domain. The timelocks themselves are constitutional (Section 22.4): the
   190→ 16757→Emergency Council cannot shorten the monetary timelock, and the DAO
   191→ 16758→cannot raise a constitutional change through a 51% vote. The councils
   192→ 16759→are distinct bodies with distinct keys — the Risk Council and the
   193→ 16760→Emergency Council operate separate 4/7 Multi-Sigs, both with hardware
   194→ 16761→wallet requirements, so that compromising one signers set does not yield
   195→ 16762→both authorities. Every action on every layer emits events and is
   196→ 16763→published (Chapter 24); the layer boundaries are exactly what Invariants
   197→ 16764→I7 and I8 reference from Section 2.6.

   199→ 16766→22.4 The Parameter Registry and Authority Matrix
   200→ 16767→
   201→ 16768→Every tunable parameter in the protocol is registered — with its
   202→ 16769→identifier, its governance layer, its current value and its admissible
   203→ 16770→envelope — in a single on-chain parameter registry [BP §14.4.2]. The
   204→ 16771→registry is the normative answer to the question of who may change what:
   205→ 16772→an update path exists if and only if the parameter is registered, and
   206→ 16773→the layer of the record determines which body, which quorum and which
   207→ 16774→timelock govern the change. Unregistered parameters fail closed — the
   208→ 16775→governance contract of Listing 14 reverts on any update to an unknown
   209→ 16776→parameter identifier — so new parameters must be deliberately registered
   210→ 16777→(a constitutional action) before they can ever be touched.
   211→ 16778→
   212→ 16779→-   Registration declares the parameter identifier, its layer
   213→ 16780→    (Constitutional, Monetary, Risk or Emergency), its initial value and
   214→ 16781→    its envelope [min, max]; registration and envelope changes follow
   215→ 16782→    the constitutional process with the 90-day timelock.
   216→ 16783→
   217→ 16784→-   Envelope discipline: proposed values must lie inside the registered
   218→ 16785→    envelope at proposal time, and are re-checked at execution time and
   219→ 16786→    again on arrival at the operations contract — three independent
   220→ 16787→    checks, so a stale or manipulated proposal cannot bypass the bounds.
   221→ 16788→
   222→ 16789→-   Timelock discipline: monetary changes execute only after 48 hours,
   223→ 16790→    risk changes only after 24 hours, and the Risk Council may veto a
   224→ 16791→    monetary proposal inside its timelock window (Section 22.6).
   225→ 16792→
   226→ 16793→-   Audit trail: every registration, proposal, execution and veto emits
   227→ 16794→    an event and is versioned with the data and methodology versions
   228→ 16795→    (Section 24.2), so the complete parameter history is reconstructable
   229→ 16796→    after the fact.

   357→ 16924→22.5 Emergency Actions
   358→ 16925→
   359→ 16926→The Emergency layer holds the protocol's fastest powers, and every one
   360→ 16927→of them is a verb — pause, force, eject, resume — rather than a setting
   361→ 16928→[BP §14.5]. Emergency authority can never change a parameter, never set
   362→ 16929→a weight and never amend the constitution; it can only halt or force the
   363→ 16930→operations the state machine would otherwise run:
   364→ 16931→
   365→ 16932→  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   366→ 16933→  Action               Description       Trigger           Authority
   367→ 16934→  -------------------- ----------------- ----------------- -----------------
   368→ 16935→  pauseMinting()       Stops all new     RR < 1.05         Automatic +
   369→ 16936→                       minting           (automatic) OR    Council
   370→ 16937→                                         Council decision  
   371→ 16938→
   372→ 16939→  pauseRedeeming()     Stops all         EMERGENCY state + Council (4/7)
   373→ 16940→                       redemptions       Council vote      
   374→ 16941→                       (extreme cases                      
   375→ 16942→                       only)                               
   376→ 16943→
   377→ 16944→  forceRebalance()     Executes an       EMERGENCY state   Council (4/7)
   378→ 16945→                       immediate                           
   379→ 16946→                       rebalance                           
   380→ 16947→
   381→ 16948→  emergencyEject()     Immediately       Issuer freeze     Council (4/7)
   382→ 16949→                       ejects a          confirmed         
   383→ 16950→                       de-pegged asset                     
   384→ 16951→
   385→ 16952→  resumeOperations()   Restores normal   Recovery          Council (4/7)
   386→ 16953→                       operations        confirmed         
   387→ 16954→  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   388→ 16955→
   389→ 16956→The trigger column shows the two-speed design. The first pause — minting
   390→ 16957→at RR < 1.05 — is automatic: the state machine applies it the moment
   391→ 16958→STRESS is entered (Section 21.4), so the protocol's first defensive move
   392→ 16959→does not wait for humans. The remaining actions are council-triggered,
   393→ 16960→because they trade user-facing continuity against risk in ways that
   394→ 16961→require judgment. Pausing redemptions is the most drastic user-facing
   395→ 16962→action in the system and requires the EMERGENCY state plus a council
   396→ 16963→vote. A forced rebalance is a discretionary override of the MARP
   397→ 16964→doctrine's justification gates and corresponds to Level 5 of the
   398→ 16965→execution hierarchy (Section 10.3). The emergency eject bypasses the
   399→ 16966→staged liquidation ladder and executes through emergencyLiquidate
   400→ 16967→(Section 21.6) when an issuer freeze is confirmed. And resumeOperations
   401→ 16968→is deliberately blocked while the state machine is still in EMERGENCY:
   402→ 16969→recovery, not decree, must end an existential state.
   403→ 16970→
   404→ 16971→Checks and balances. Every emergency action requires the 4/7 Emergency
   405→ 16972→Council Multi-Sig with hardware wallet requirements, emits an on-chain
   406→ 16973→event, and is reported through the transparency layer (Chapter 24) and
   407→ 16974→the honest-status declaration (Section 25). The oracle layer's circuit
   408→ 16975→breakers (Chapter 17) engage automatically in EMERGENCY, so price-trust
   409→ 16976→failures and solvency failures brake together. The state machine
   410→ 16977→continues to run underneath the council's actions: an emergency pause
   411→ 16978→does not freeze RR/LCR reporting, state transitions or the recovery
   412→ 16979→clock, so the council cannot use a pause to obscure the condition it is
   413→ 16980→pausing about. Emergency power, in short, is instant but narrow,
   414→ 16981→recorded, and reversible only by recovery — the exact trade the
   415→ 16982→four-layer hierarchy of Section 22.3 is designed to force.

   417→ 16984→22.6 Smart Contract Implementation — Governance and Emergency Functions
   418→ 16985→
   419→ 16986→The governance registry is the authorization plane of the protocol [BP
   420→ 16987→§14.6.4–14.6.5]. It owns the parameter registry and the proposal flow:
   421→ 16988→the DAO proposes and executes monetary changes across a 48-hour
   422→ 16989→timelock, the Risk Council owns the 24-hour risk path and holds a veto
   423→ 16990→over monetary proposals inside their window, the Constitutional Council
   424→ 16991→registers parameters and their envelopes, and the Emergency Council
   425→ 16992→dispatches the pause, force and resume actions of Section 22.5. Every
   426→ 16993→executed update is forwarded to the operations contract of Listing 13
   427→ 16994→through the IOperations interface, and the operations contract
   428→ 16995→re-verifies its caller before applying the value — authorization and
   429→ 16996→execution each check the other. The emergency eject is the single
   430→ 16997→exception to the dispatch pattern: emergencyLiquidate is signed directly
   431→ 16998→by the Emergency Council on the operations contract (Section 21.6),
   432→ 16999→exactly as in the source module. The onlyDAO modifier is implemented by
   433→ 17000→a DAO contract with 51% quorum, and the emergency modifier by a 4/7
   434→ 17001→Multi-Sig with hardware wallet requirements [BP §14].
